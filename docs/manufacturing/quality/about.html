About Quality Assurance

Quality Event and Corrective Action Management: one connected path through NCR, MRB, CAPA and SCAR, with lot traceability. OutSystems module VS_MFG_QualityAssurance.

One Quality Event behind everything

NCR, MRB, CAPA and SCAR aren’t four separate applications. Every quality issue starts from, or is linked to, a Quality Event, and the other records hang off it. That’s what lets anyone open one issue and see its containment, its lots, its supplier and its corrective actions in one place.

The full path

Quality issue found→Quality Event→NCR→Containment→Lot impact analysis→MRB→Disposition→SCAR→CAPA→Corrective actions→Effectiveness check→Closure

Not every event needs every stage. Typical paths:

  • Minor defect: Quality Event → NCR → MRB → Disposition → Close
  • Supplier issue: Quality Event → NCR → Containment → SCAR → Supplier response → Verification → Close
  • Systemic problem: Quality Event → NCR → Containment → CAPA → Root cause → Corrective actions → Effectiveness check → Close
  • Major supplier or systemic problem: Quality Event → NCR → Lot trace → MRB + SCAR + CAPA → Effectiveness check → Close

Modules and their lifecycles

Quality Event

Records the issue when it’s found: the problem, where it was found, the product, material and lot, quantities, risk and immediate actions. Its lifecycle actions are Assign, Submit, Contain, Start Investigation, Close and Reopen. Status list to be confirmed.

NCR: Nonconformance Report

Formally documents material, product or process conditions that don’t meet requirements. Created directly or from a Quality Event.

Draft→Open→Containment→Awaiting MRB→Verification→Disposition Approved→Closed

MRB: Material Review Board

Reviews nonconforming material and approves or rejects the proposed disposition.

Draft→Under Review→Disposition→Approval→Completed

CAPA: Corrective and Preventive Action

Finds and fixes the root cause of a recurring or systemic problem, then checks the fix worked.

Draft→Investigation→Root Cause→Action Plan→Implementation→Effectiveness→Closed
  • Before Action Plan: the investigation summary, confirmed root cause and escape point are required.
  • Before closing: the problem statement, business impact, scope and effectiveness result are required.
  • If the effectiveness check finds the fix not effective, the CAPA goes back to Investigation.

SCAR: Supplier Corrective Action Request

Asks a supplier to find and fix the cause of a problem with their material. The supplier answers with an 8D response, step by step.

Draft→Issued→Supplier Acknowledged→Supplier Response→Manufacturer Review→Implementation→Verification→Closed
  • A SCAR can be accepted only when every 8D step is Accepted.

Lot Trace

One of the main differentiators. Starting from any lot:

  • Forward trace answers “where did this defective material go?”: supplier lot → receipt → internal lot → work orders → WIP lots → finished-goods lots → shipments → customers.
  • Backward trace goes from a finished lot to its work order, operations, component and raw-material lots, supplier lots, purchase orders and supplier.
  • Where used lists every production order, child lot, finished good, shipment and customer for a lot.
  • Genealogy is a recursive parent/child relationship, so it handles splits, merges, rework, repackaging, subassemblies and multi-level BOMs. The trace screen shows it as a graph where every node opens its details.

Supporting records

Quality holds, containment actions, actions, root cause analysis (5 Whys), effectiveness checks, files, comments, approvals and an audit log support every module.

Roles

  • Operator / Production User: creates Quality Events, identifies affected material and lots, uploads photos, records immediate containment, completes assigned actions. Sees events for their own production area. Doesn’t normally approve NCR, MRB, CAPA or SCAR closure.
  • Quality Inspector: creates and edits Quality Events and NCRs, records inspection findings, defect codes and affected quantities, places material holds, traces lots, recommends MRB dispositions.
  • Quality Engineer: runs NCR investigations and lot-impact analysis, starts CAPAs and SCARs, does root-cause analysis, defines corrective actions, requests MRB review, performs effectiveness checks, recommends closure.
  • Supplier Quality Engineer: creates and manages SCARs, works with suppliers, accepts or rejects supplier corrective-action plans, tracks supplier quality.
  • Supplier User: the supplier’s side of a SCAR. Permissions to be confirmed.
  • MRB Member: from quality, engineering, manufacturing, supply chain, procurement or operations. Reviews nonconforming material and approves or rejects dispositions.
  • Quality Manager: approves CAPAs, major NCR closures and MRB decisions (within authorization thresholds), reviews overdue events, reopens closed events, sees organization-wide metrics.
  • System Administrator: configures sites, plants, departments, products, materials, defect codes, event types, risk classifications, disposition types, CAPA and root-cause categories, workflow, approval, notification and escalation rules, and user roles.

Terms

  • 8D: the eight-discipline problem-solving format suppliers use to answer a SCAR.
  • Containment: immediate steps that stop bad material moving further (holds, 100% inspection, blocked shipments) while the cause is found.
  • Disposition: what happens to nonconforming material, such as use as is, rework, return to supplier or scrap.
  • Escape point: the point in the process where the defect should have been caught but wasn’t.
  • Lot / supplier lot: a traceable batch of material. A supplier lot is the supplier’s own number for what they shipped.
  • Customer: who receives finished goods from a shipment.
